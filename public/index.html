Model notes

A simplified 3.0-second earthquake shakes the ground and starts the tower moving. After that, the foundation stops and the graph shows free motion. Sway means roof displacement relative to the foundation, x - y.

T = 2*pi*sqrt(L/g) and L = g*(T/2*pi)^2

Tower height and mass use a classroom calibration: taller or heavier towers have longer natural periods, kept within a range suited to stopwatch measurements. The drawing is schematic; taller towers are drawn taller and heavier towers have thicker columns. Keep the same tower settings when comparing TMD designs.

Two hydraulic dampers remove mechanical energy from pendulum motion. At zero damping, the hydraulic dampers are removed.

Compare mode uses identical towers and the same earthquake with independent TMD settings. Start, Pause, and Reset apply to both. The graph uses the same time and sway axes for both traces.

One pendulum wall hit collapses that tower in every difficulty mode. Low damping and a small TMD mass can produce hits. The drawing limits displayed swing to leave room for hydraulic housings; the wall-hit label briefly highlights real impacts. Impact losses are separate from hydraulic thermal energy.

Danger rule: Easy has no danger-zone collapse. Medium fails at 3.0 s of accumulated danger time. Hard fails at 2.5 s. Danger time starts after the earthquake and builds while roof sway is beyond the tower's game sway limit: m. This tolerance varies by tower to keep challenges achievable; it is not a real structural prediction.
